title: "Fake authority video call"
longTitle: 'Is this police video call accusing me a scam? Yes — how the fake-authority call works'
description: "The fake authority video call scam explained: a supposed officer accuses you on video, orders isolation, and demands transfers for verification. Warning signs and what to do."
tag: "Intimidation"
intro: "You get a video call from someone in uniform, or a caller showing a badge and case file, accusing you of a parcel with drugs, money laundering, or an arrest warrant in your name. They order you to stay isolated on camera and transfer money to a safe account for verification. Real police never interrogate or clear cases over video calls — the whole setup is staged to panic you into paying."
looksLike:
  - "\"This is Officer Sharma, CBI. Your Aadhaar is linked to a parcel with drugs seized at the airport. You are under digital arrest.\""
  - "A video call showing a person in uniform with a badge, with fake documents and a case number on screen"
  - "\"Transfer the amount to this verification account and do not disconnect the call or tell anyone — it will affect the investigation.\""
warningSigns:
  - "The caller accuses you of a serious crime out of nowhere and refuses to let you hang up or talk to family."
  - "They demand secrecy and isolation — real officers never forbid you from contacting a lawyer or family."
  - "They ask for money transfers for verification, bail, or case closure over the phone."
  - "The call happens on Skype, WhatsApp, or Telegram video instead of an official station visit with written notice."
whyItWorks: "Fear of arrest shuts down clear thinking, and the uniform, badge, and case papers make the lie feel official. Forced isolation removes the one thing that would break the spell: a second opinion from family or real police."
whatToDo:
  - "Hang up immediately — do not stay on the line, transfer money, or share bank details."
  - "Tell a family member what happened and call your local police on their publicly listed number."
  - "Save screenshots, numbers, and payment details, then report to your national cybercrime service."
verify: "Real investigations use written summons and in-person visits, never video-call arrests or verification transfers. Look up your local police number yourself and call to confirm — never call back the number on screen. Report to the FBI at ic3.gov or your national cybercrime helpline."
faqs:
  - q: "What is digital arrest? Is it real?"
    a: "No. There is no such legal procedure. It is a scammer invention where fake officers keep you on video and demand money. Real police never arrest or interrogate over video calls."
  - q: "They showed my ID details and a case file — how?"
    a: "Personal details leak from data breaches and fake documents are easy to forge. A real case is served in writing, not displayed on a WhatsApp video call."
  - q: "They told me not to tell anyone — what should I do?"
    a: "That secrecy order is the scam. Hang up, tell someone you trust immediately, and call your local police on their official number to confirm there is no case."
