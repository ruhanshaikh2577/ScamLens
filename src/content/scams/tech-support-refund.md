title: "Tech support / refund scam"
longTitle: 'Is this virus warning or refund call real? How fake tech-support and over-refund scams work'
description: "Tech-support scam explained: fake virus pop-ups and refund calls lead to remote access and a staged over-refund you must return via cash, crypto, or gift cards."
tag: "Tech support"
region: "global"
intro: "A pop-up or call warns your device has a virus, or an invoice says your antivirus subscription renewed. The 'technician' asks for remote access to fix it, then shows a fake over-refund and pressures you to return the difference via cash, crypto, or gift cards. The refund never existed — your money is the only transfer. Variants include Microsoft/Apple pop-ups plus cash-by-courier (US), BT/Microsoft cold calls (UK), Defender pop-ups (EU), the same call-centre scripts (India), and suporte Microsoft via WhatsApp (Brazil)."
looksLike:
  - "Full-screen pop-up: 'Virus detected — call Microsoft support at 1-XXX now' with a loud alarm and a support number."
  - "Email invoice: 'Your antivirus renewed for $499 — call to cancel and get a refund,' leading to a remote-access app install."
  - "'We refunded $500 instead of $50 — return the difference via gift cards / wire / cash pickup before police action.'"
warningSigns:
  - "Unsolicited virus warning with a phone number — real Microsoft, Apple, and Google never do this."
  - "Asks you to install remote-access software (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) or open online banking on screen."
  - "Refund shown only on a typed screen or edited bank page, then urgency to return the 'extra' via gift cards, crypto, or cash."
  - "Renewal invoice for software you never bought, with a callback number instead of an official account page."
whyItWorks:
  "Fear of a hacked device plus the authority of a big-brand name makes people obey. Remote access looks professional, and the fake over-refund flips the victim into feeling indebted — returning 'their money' feels honest, which hides that it is theft."
whatToDo:
  - "Hang up and close the pop-up (use Task Manager / Force Quit if stuck). Do not install remote-access apps or share codes."
  - "If you gave access or paid, call your bank immediately, uninstall the remote tool, and run a real antivirus scan."
  - "Report: US reportfraud.ftc.gov and FBI IC3, UK actionfraud.police.uk, India 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in."
verify:
  "Check subscriptions only inside your official account (microsoft.com, apple.com) reached by typing the address yourself. Real support never cold-calls about viruses and never asks for gift cards, crypto, or cash to process a refund."
faqs:
  - q: "Did Microsoft really detect a virus on my computer?"
    a: "No. Browser pop-ups with a support number are fake. Close the tab, run your installed antivirus, and never call the number on the pop-up."
  - q: "Why do they show an over-refund on my screen?"
    a: "They edit the page or ask you to type the amount, making it look like they sent too much. No money moved — the 'return' you send is the theft."
  - q: "I gave remote access — what now?"
    a: "Disconnect internet, uninstall the remote app, call your bank, change passwords from a clean device, and report at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), actionfraud.police.uk (UK), or 1930 (India)."
