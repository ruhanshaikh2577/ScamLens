title: "Romance / relationship scam"
longTitle: 'Is this online relationship a scam? How romance grooming and sextortion work'
description: "Romance scam explained: long grooming builds trust, then an investment tip, emergency cash request, or explicit-image blackmail. Warning signs and verification steps."
tag: "Relationships"
region: "global"
intro: "Someone you met on a dating app or social media chats daily for weeks, then the story pivots: a can't-miss investment tip, an emergency needing cash or gift cards, or threats to leak explicit photos. The affection is the setup — the money request is the scam. Variants include dating-app crypto pivots (US), romance-to-investment introductions (UK/EU), dating-chat plus sextortion demands (India), and golpe do amor on Tinder/Instagram (Brazil)."
looksLike:
  - "Daily messages for weeks, then: 'My uncle trades crypto — I can show you, just start with $200 on this platform.'"
  - "'I need emergency surgery money / a flight to meet you — send via wire or gift cards, don't tell anyone.'"
  - "'Pay now or I send your explicit photos to your family' — with a screenshot to prove they have them."
warningSigns:
  - "Refuses video calls or in-person meetings, with endless excuses while affection escalates fast."
  - "Moves chat off the dating app quickly and introduces money: investments, fees, emergencies."
  - "Investment 'platform' is a link they sent, showing growing balances you can never withdraw without fees."
  - "Sextortion threats demanding secrecy and payment by crypto, gift card, or wire within hours."
whyItWorks:
  "Weeks of attention create real emotional trust, which overrides financial caution. The request arrives framed as help, partnership, or shame-avoidance — not as a transaction. By the time money is mentioned, questioning the person feels like betraying the relationship."
whatToDo:
  - "Stop sending money and stop complying with threats — paying sextortion demands never ends them."
  - "Screenshot profiles, chats, payment addresses, and transaction IDs; preserve URLs before blocking."
  - "Report: US reportfraud.ftc.gov and FBI IC3, UK actionfraud.police.uk, India 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in."
verify:
  "Reverse-image-search their photos, insist on a live video call, and never invest via a link someone sent you. Real advisers are registered and never recruit through dating chats; real emergencies can be verified through family or official channels — not secrecy."
faqs:
  - q: "How do I know an online relationship is a scam?"
    a: "If affection pivots to money — an investment tip, emergency cash, or fees to meet — it is a scam. Real partners do not route you to trading links or demand secrecy about payments."
  - q: "They threaten to leak my photos — should I pay?"
    a: "No. Do not pay, do not send more images, screenshot everything, block the account, and report at cybercrime.gov.in (India), reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), or actionfraud.police.uk (UK)."
  - q: "The trading site shows my profit growing — is it real?"
    a: "No. Scam dashboards show fake balances; withdrawals are blocked with fee or tax excuses. Never deposit via a link from a dating contact."
