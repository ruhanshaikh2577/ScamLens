title: "Tax / government refund scam"
longTitle: 'Is "tax refund pending" or "pay now or face arrest" a scam? How tax-office impersonation works'
description: "Tax-agency impersonation explained: fake refund offers and arrest threats that steal login and bank details, with warning signs and official verification steps."
tag: "Government"
region: "global"
intro: "A text, email or call claims to be your tax office — you are owed a refund, or you owe tax and face arrest. Both versions push you to a link, a callback number, or a payment to 'verify' identity. Real tax agencies do not demand bank logins, card details or urgent payments this way. Variants include IRS refund/arrest threats (US), HMRC rebate or lawsuit threats (UK), CRA e-transfer refund (Canada), Impôts/Ameli refund messages (France), Receita Federal CPF-pending messages (Brazil), and Income Tax refund links (India)."
looksLike:
  - "'IRS: You are eligible for a $1,420 tax refund. Claim here: irs-refund-claim.com' — or the reverse: 'Pay back taxes now or face arrest within 24 hours.'"
  - "'HMRC: You are due a £342 tax rebate. Claim via this link' — sender is not gov.uk and asks for card and sort code."
  - "'CRA: You have an e-transfer refund waiting — deposit it here' or 'Receita Federal: regularize seu CPF para liberar a restituição' with a boleto/taxa link."
warningSigns:
  - "Refund you never filed for, or a tax debt with an arrest threat and a same-day deadline."
  - "Link or sender domain does not match the official tax portal (irs.gov, gov.uk, canada.ca, gov.br/receitafederal, impots.gouv.fr, incometax.gov.in)."
  - "Asks for bank login, card number, CVV, OTP, or payment by gift card, wire, or crypto to receive or clear tax."
  - "Generic greeting ('Dear taxpayer'), spoofed caller ID, and orders not to tell family or call your accountant."
whyItWorks:
  "Tax is confusing and intimidating everywhere, so a message from 'the tax office' borrows instant authority. The refund version exploits wishful thinking; the arrest version exploits fear. Both create urgency so you click or pay before checking the real portal, where no such refund or case exists."
whatToDo:
  - "Do not click, call back, or pay. Close the message and open the tax portal yourself by typing its known address."
  - "If you entered bank or login details, call your bank immediately, change the password, and enable two-factor authentication."
  - "Report it: US reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK actionfraud.police.uk and phishing@hmrc.gov.uk, India 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in."
verify:
  "Check refunds or dues only inside your official tax account reached by typing the address yourself (irs.gov, gov.uk Personal tax account, canada.ca/CRAMyAccount, gov.br/receitafederal, impots.gouv.fr, incometax.gov.in). Real notices also arrive by post or inside the portal — never as a refund link demanding card details."
faqs:
  - q: "Does the tax office offer refunds by text or email link?"
    a: "No. Real refunds are claimed inside your official tax account or sent per local process — never via a texted link asking for card, login, or OTP. Any refund link is phishing."
  - q: "Can I be arrested for unpaid tax over a call?"
    a: "No. Tax agencies do not threaten immediate arrest by phone or text, and never demand gift cards, wire, or crypto. Hang up and verify via the official portal."
  - q: "I entered my bank details — what now?"
    a: "Call your bank to block the card, change portal passwords, and report at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), actionfraud.police.uk (UK), or 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in (India)."
