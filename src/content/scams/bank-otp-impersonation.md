title: "Bank fraud-team OTP call"
longTitle: 'Is a call from "your bank fraud team" asking for an OTP a scam? Yes — here is how it works'
description: "The bank-impersonation OTP scam explained: fake fraud-team calls and SMS that extract OTPs or trigger collect-request transfers, regional variants, and how to verify safely."
tag: "Banking"
region: "global"
intro: "Someone calls or texts claiming to be your bank's fraud team warning of suspicious activity. They ask you to read out the OTP just sent to your phone — or to approve a request in your payment app — to “block” the fraud. In reality the OTP or approval authorises the theft: a Zelle transfer (US), an authorised-push payment (UK), a Pix transfer or courier cash pickup (Brazil), or a UPI collect request (India). Real bank staff never ask for your OTP."
looksLike:
  - "“This is Chase fraud prevention — a $1,800 Zelle transfer was attempted. Read back the code we just sent to cancel it.”"
  - "“Bank investigator here — move your money to this safe account while we investigate. Do not tell your branch.”"
  - A UPI collect request or “receive money” PIN prompt in your payments app arriving mid-call, framed as a refund to accept
warningSigns:
  - "Any request for an OTP, PIN or approval in your banking app — genuine staff never need these."
  - "Urgency plus secrecy: “act now, don't tell anyone, don't visit the branch.”"
  - "Caller ID or SMS sender can be spoofed — the display name matching your bank proves nothing."
  - "Instructions to approve a request, move money to a “safe account”, or hand cash to a courier — banks never operate this way."
whyItWorks: "The caller borrows the bank's authority and pairs it with fear of losing money, so victims comply before thinking — the same script localised everywhere (Chase/BofA plus Zelle in the US, bank-investigator plus authorised-push payments in the UK, falsa central plus Pix or motoboy pickup in Brazil, fake KYC plus UPI collect in India). Because the victim “approves” the transfer themselves, the money moves instantly and is far harder to recover."
whatToDo:
  - "Hang up. Never read out an OTP or approve a payment request from an incoming call or SMS."
  - "Call your bank yourself using the number on your card or its official app, and check your accounts there."
  - "If you approved something, call your bank immediately to freeze the transfer, then report it (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in)."
verify: "End the call and log in to your bank's official app, or call the helpline number printed on your card — never a number the caller gives you. Any real fraud alert will be visible in your account or confirmed by the bank's own team on a call you initiated."
faqs:
  - q: "Will my bank ever ask for my OTP?"
    a: "No. No genuine bank employee — fraud team or otherwise — ever asks for your OTP, PIN or app approval. Anyone who does is a scammer, regardless of what the caller ID shows."
  - q: "What is a UPI collect request / payment approval trap?"
    a: "It is a request sent to your payments app that, when you enter your PIN, sends money from your account instead of receiving it. Scammers frame it as a refund or verification — never enter your PIN on a request you did not initiate."
  - q: "I already shared the OTP — what now?"
    a: "Call your bank immediately to freeze the transaction and block your accounts, change your banking passwords, then report it (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in)."
