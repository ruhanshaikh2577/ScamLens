title: "Marketplace QR / payment-proof scam"
longTitle: 'Is a buyer''s QR code or "payment screenshot" on a marketplace a scam? Often yes — here is how it works'
description: "The marketplace QR and fake payment-proof scam explained: off-platform buyers, fake screenshots and collect-request QRs, regional variants, and how to trade safely."
tag: "Payments"
region: "global"
intro: "A buyer from a marketplace chat moves you off-platform, then “pays” with a screenshot of a transfer — or sends a QR code or payment link for you to scan to “receive” the money. If you ship the goods on the strength of the screenshot, no money ever arrives. If you scan the QR and enter your PIN, you authorise a collect request — money leaves your account instead of entering it. The pattern is identical worldwide: Facebook/Zelle overpay (US), Vinted/eBay bank-link (UK), fake Pix receipt on OLX/Mercado Livre (Brazil), QR-UPI collect on OLX (India), faux lien Leboncoin (France)."
looksLike:
  - "“I overpaid by $200 via Zelle — refund the difference and keep extra for your trouble. Here's the screenshot.”"
  - "“Scan this QR in your UPI app to receive my payment” — the app shows a collect request asking for your PIN."
  - "“Pay the courier fee through this link so I can complete the purchase” on a Vinted, eBay or Leboncoin chat."
warningSigns:
  - "Buyer insists on leaving the platform's chat or payment system for WhatsApp, SMS or email."
  - "Payment “proof” is a screenshot instead of money visible in your own account or app."
  - "Overpayment plus a refund request — the original payment always bounces or never existed."
  - "Any QR, link or request that asks you to enter your PIN to “receive” money — receiving never needs your PIN."
whyItWorks: "Sellers are eager to close the deal and trust what looks like a bank confirmation, so a convincing screenshot short-circuits the one check that matters (money in your own account). The QR variant exploits a real UI confusion: scanning and entering a PIN feels like receiving, but on UPI-style systems it authorises a payment. Moving off-platform strips away the marketplace's protections at exactly the wrong moment."
whatToDo:
  - "Keep chat and payment inside the marketplace. Ship only after the money is visible in your own account — never on a screenshot."
  - "Never scan a buyer's QR or enter your PIN to “receive” money. To receive, you share your ID — you enter nothing."
  - "If you paid or shipped, contact your bank or payments app immediately, report the profile on the marketplace, and file at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), actionfraud.police.uk (UK), or 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in (India)."
verify: "Check your own bank or payments app balance and transaction history — not the buyer's screenshot. For marketplace sales, confirm the order status inside the platform's official app or site before shipping anything."
faqs:
  - q: "Is a payment screenshot enough proof to ship?"
    a: "No. Screenshots are trivially faked. Ship only when the funds appear in your own bank account or inside the marketplace's official order status — never on the strength of an image the buyer sent."
  - q: "Do I ever need to scan a QR or enter my PIN to receive money?"
    a: "No. Receiving money never requires your PIN or approving a collect request. If scanning asks for your PIN, it is a payment you are authorising — cancel it."
  - q: "The buyer overpaid and asks for a refund of the difference — is that safe?"
    a: "No, that is the overpayment trap. The original transfer will bounce or never arrives, while your “refund” is real money leaving your account. Cancel the sale and keep everything inside the platform."
