title: "Unpaid toll SMS"
longTitle: 'Is an "unpaid toll + late fine" text a scam? Yes — here is how it works'
description: "The unpaid-toll SMS scam explained: toll-brand clones harvesting card and OTP data, regional variants, warning signs, and how to verify toll dues safely."
tag: "Toll & transit"
region: "global"
intro: "A text claims you owe an unpaid toll plus a growing late fine, with a link to settle it now. The link opens a clone of a toll brand — E-ZPass, SunPass or FasTrak (US), Ulys, e-TOLL or Telepass (EU), Sem Parar (Brazil), FASTag (India) or 407 ETR (Canada) — that harvests your card number and the OTP sent to your phone. Real toll operators never bill you this way over SMS."
looksLike:
  - "“E-ZPass: you have an unpaid toll of $4.60 plus $25 late fine. Pay within 12h to avoid penalties: ezpass-pay.com”"
  - "“FASTag KYC suspended — pay toll dues now: bit.ly/fastag-due”"
  - A toll-brand clone page showing your “outstanding balance” and a form asking for card details plus the OTP
warningSigns:
  - "Unsolicited SMS about tolls on roads or tags you never used, with a payment link."
  - "The domain is not the toll operator's official site — hyphenated look-alikes and shorteners."
  - "Escalating fine plus a hours-long deadline to force instant payment."
  - "The page asks for full card details and an OTP — toll sites never collect payment this way by text."
whyItWorks: "Tolling is fragmented and confusing — drivers genuinely don't know which operator covers which road, so any official-looking brand and balance feels plausible (E-ZPass/SunPass/FasTrak, Ulys/e-TOLL/Telepass, Sem Parar, FASTag, 407 ETR). The fine framing turns a tiny amount into an urgent threat, and the clone page converts one careless payment into reusable card data plus an OTP that authorises larger theft."
whatToDo:
  - "Do not click the link. Check dues only in the toll operator's official app or on its known website typed by you."
  - "If you entered card details, call your card issuer to block the card and dispute the charge."
  - "Report the smish: forward to 7726 (SPAM) where supported, file at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US) or actionfraud.police.uk (UK), and in India call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in."
verify: "Log in to your toll account on the operator's official app or website (typed yourself) and check the dues or violations section. Any genuine unpaid toll appears there — never only inside a texted link."
faqs:
  - q: "Do toll operators really text payment links for unpaid tolls?"
    a: "No. Real operators (E-ZPass, SunPass, FasTrak, Telepass, FASTag issuers, 407 ETR and others) notify through your account or official mail and never demand card-plus-OTP payment via an SMS link."
  - q: "How do I check a real toll due?"
    a: "Open the operator's official app or type its official website yourself and check the dues section with your vehicle or account number. Ignore any link in the text."
  - q: "I paid through the link — what now?"
    a: "Call your card issuer immediately to block the card and dispute the charge, watch for follow-up charges, and report the text (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in)."
