title: "Family emergency message"
longTitle: 'Is an urgent "it''s me, new number — send money now" message a scam? Assume yes — here is how it works'
description: "The family-emergency message scam explained: new-number, arrest and accident pretexts, AI voice and video-call variants, and how to verify before sending money."
tag: "Messaging"
region: "global"
intro: "A message claims to be your child, grandchild or parent — new number, arrested, in an accident — and demands money immediately, no questions, no calls. The pretext is localised everywhere (grandparent plus AI-cloned voice in the US, “Hi Mum/Hi Dad” WhatsApp in the UK, “allo maman” in France, “mãe, troquei de número” plus Pix in Brazil, fake-police “digital arrest” video calls in India), but the mechanism is identical: panic plus secrecy so you pay before verifying."
looksLike:
  - "“Hi Mum, it's me — dropped my phone, this is my new number. Please don't call, I'm in a meeting. Can you transfer £800 urgently?”"
  - "“Mãe, troquei de número. Sofri um acidente e preciso de um Pix agora — não conta pro pai.”"
  - "A video call from “police” saying your child is in custody and you must pay to avoid jail — cameras, uniforms and case numbers included."
warningSigns:
  - "A new number plus “don't call, only text” — designed to block the one check that kills the scam."
  - "Extreme urgency with secrecy: “now, tell no one, don't call dad/the bank.”"
  - "Payment by instant rails — transfer, Pix, gift cards, crypto — with no verifiable case, hospital or station details."
  - "AI voice or video pressure: a familiar-sounding voice or an official-looking video call that refuses independent callbacks."
whyItWorks: "Parental and grandparental love overrides scepticism — the message hijacks the moments when thinking clearly is hardest. Cloned voices and fake video calls supply just enough “proof” to satisfy a panicked brain, and the secrecy instruction isolates the victim from the family member who could debunk it in one call."
whatToDo:
  - "Stop and call your family member on their real, saved number — or through another relative. Never reply-only on the new number."
  - "Ask a question only the real person could answer, and independently call the police station, hospital or school on its public number."
  - "Never transfer under time pressure. If you paid, call your bank immediately, then report (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in)."
verify: "Hang up and dial back on a number you already trust — the saved contact, the school office, the police station's public line. Real police and hospitals accept callbacks and give verifiable case details; scammers forbid them."
faqs:
  - q: "The voice really sounded like my grandchild — could it still be fake?"
    a: "Yes. AI voice cloning needs only seconds of audio from social media. Treat any urgent money demand as unverified until you reach the person on their real number or through another family member."
  - q: "What is a “digital arrest” video call?"
    a: "Scammers pose as police on video, claim you or a relative face arrest, and demand money to “settle the case.” Real police never arrest over video call or demand payment to avoid custody — hang up and call the station's public number."
  - q: "I already transferred the money — what now?"
    a: "Call your bank immediately to try to freeze or recall the transfer, then report it (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in) with screenshots and transaction IDs."
