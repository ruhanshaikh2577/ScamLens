title: "Missed delivery / customs fee"
longTitle: 'Is "missed delivery — pay a fee to release your parcel" a scam SMS? Yes — here is how it works'
description: "The missed-delivery / customs-fee SMS scam explained: fake tracking pages harvesting card details, regional variants, warning signs, and how to verify a parcel safely."
tag: "Delivery"
region: "global"
intro: "A text claims your parcel missed delivery or is held at customs, and a small fee will release it. The link opens a fake tracking page — branded like USPS, Royal Mail, DHL, Correios, India Post or Sagawa depending on your country — that asks for your address and full card details. The fee is fake; the page exists only to harvest your card and personal data for bigger fraud."
looksLike:
  - "“USPS: your parcel could not be delivered. Pay a $2.10 redelivery fee within 24h: usps-track-help.com”"
  - "“Royal Mail: customs fee due on your item. Confirm address and pay now: bit.ly/rm-release”"
  - A fake tracking page with the carrier's logo, a progress bar stuck at “held”, and a form asking for card number, expiry and CVV
warningSigns:
  - "Unsolicited SMS about a parcel you never ordered or cannot tie to a real tracking number."
  - "The link domain is not the carrier's official site — look-alikes, misspellings and URL shorteners."
  - "A tiny fee plus full card details — real carriers never collect card data over an SMS link."
  - "Countdown pressure (“24 hours or returned to sender”) to stop you checking the official app."
whyItWorks: "Almost everyone shops online, so a delivery problem feels plausible in any country — the same template just swaps the brand (USPS, Royal Mail, DHL/La Poste, Correios, India Post/DTDC, Sagawa). The fee is trivially small so victims pay without inspecting the domain, and the fake tracking page looks convincing enough that handing over card details feels like routine checkout."
whatToDo:
  - "Do not click the link. Open the carrier's official app or type its known website yourself and track with your real tracking number."
  - "If you entered card details, call your card issuer to block the card and dispute the charge, then monitor for follow-up charges."
  - "Report the text: forward to 7726 (SPAM) where supported, file at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US) or actionfraud.police.uk (UK), and in India call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in."
verify: "Track only with the tracking number from your actual order confirmation, entered on the carrier's official site or app. Carriers list customs or redelivery fees inside your tracking record — never via a texted payment link."
faqs:
  - q: "Do carriers really text links to pay customs or redelivery fees?"
    a: "No. Real carriers (USPS, Royal Mail, DHL, Correios, India Post and others) never ask you to pay fees or enter card details through an SMS link. Any fee is shown inside your official tracking record when you look it up yourself."
  - q: "How do I check a delivery notice safely?"
    a: "Ignore the link. Type the carrier's official website yourself or open its app, and enter the tracking number from your order confirmation. If the tracking record shows nothing held, the message was fake."
  - q: "I paid the fee — what should I do now?"
    a: "Call your card issuer immediately to block the card and dispute the charge, watch for larger follow-up charges, and report the message (US: reportfraud.ftc.gov, UK: actionfraud.police.uk, India: 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in)."
